Founding member survey

Tell us where crush uncertainty gets difficult.

This short survey helps us understand moments when someone likes a specific person but feels unsure what to say, when to say it, or how to read the response.

Focused feedback

Short and helpful.

Answer what you can. The goal is to learn how people think through crush-related uncertainty before acting, while avoiding names or unnecessary identifying details beyond your founding email link.

Your answers help shape product direction, language, and early crush-related use cases.

1. Tell me about the last time you felt uncertain about a crush.

Use a general example. Please avoid names or identifying details.

2. What was your relationship to this person?

Keep it broad, such as classmate, coworker, friend, dating-app match, old friend, or someone you recently met.

3. What happened, and what made the crush situation hard to read?

Share the context, recent signals, timing, and why the next move felt uncertain.

4. What choices were you considering?

List possible actions, including texting first, waiting, asking to meet, being direct, staying playful, or doing nothing.

5. What response or consequence were you most worried about?

Tell us what you feared could happen if your next move landed wrong.

6. What outcome were you hoping for?

Describe what you wanted to happen with your crush and what feeling you wanted to protect.

7. What did you do at the time to handle the uncertainty?

Tell us what action you took, even if the action was waiting, rewriting the message, or doing nothing.

8. What support, tools, advice, or habits helped you think it through?

Include anything that shaped how you decided what to say or whether to act.

9. What would have made Syntrae useful in that crush moment?

Tell us what kind of reply examples, timing comparison, tone check, or next-message guidance would have helped.

10. If you know somebody dealing with crush uncertainty, please share Syntrae with them. It helps us a lot.

A small share can help someone think through the message before the moment becomes awkward.
